## Men & Machines

The sun’s final rays peek over the horizon for the day. An etude of orange and pink suspends itself against the firmament, waiting for space and its stars to take watch. It follows the lone sentinel, Veryn.

He exits Central Park and saunters down Fifth Avenue, his loafers scraping across the littered concrete. A few minutes of walking leads him to a building taller than his neck can tolerate. “HYLIC PARTNERS,” the channel letters shout, glowing yellow on the overhead cabinet sign. His choice of a navy-blue suit, navy-blue pants, and red tie fits with that of the ashen employees inside and outside, who only pay him a passing glance as he waits in line to scan his ID.

Two concierges at the front desk buzz through the lobby’s chatter. One fashions a pair of smartglasses, while the other is blindfolded.

“So, how’d that ‘dinner and a show’ thing with Daniel go?” the blindfolded miss queries. Her nametag reads as “Rae.”

The bespectacled lass, Les, pouts, "Remember when you went out with that one guy a few months ago? You two were watching the duel, and then, like, that accident happened, right?”

“Yeah?”

“Right, it was like that, but it was a stray plasma ball instead. Went straight through the big window in front of us.”

“Why can’t they just make the windows stronger? Engineers these days are so lazy\!”

“I know, right? It was super annoying, but whatever. Danny asked to go home after that, and I was *so* *bored* for the rest of the day.”

“Men these days\! Ugh, all of them should get their genes reprogrammed or something.”

“I know, right? I’m pretty sure he has the money for that too…”

The sound of footsteps draws their attention from each other. The source grows closer without adopting a rhythm, deaf to the drumbeat that the workers are playing.

Les pokes her head out from behind her computer. “Rae, it’s that guy again,” she whispers.

Rae rests her head on Les’s, snickering. “What do you think he’s here for today, Les?”

“Definitely not shadowing another meeting. His clothes are wrinklier than your bedsheets.”

Les adjusts her glasses and leans a bit forward, her chest typing nonsense like a rowdy cat with the keyboard in front of her. She points her head to the entrance, though her pupils follow the navy-blue man in her periphery. 

“His shirt is all misbuttoned,” Les comments, shaking her head. “I think he forgot how a button-down works.”

Rae giggles, “Eight bucks says he won’t jump off the roof.”

Les scoffs, “Nine says he will\!”

Veryn wades through the sea of workers, stopping at the elevator. Everyone surrounding him carries themselves with the same, gray uniform. The person at his port cackles at a colleague’s shanty. The person at his starboard gapes at the LED lighting, arms crossed. Each expression shifts in their own directions, though they all end up in a frown.

When the elevator bell dings and doors slide open, hundreds of suited men and women spill out into the lobby. Veryn slips in and presses a button labeled “103.” Within a minute, the doors slide shut and the cab shoots upward to the man’s destination. Amidst the minute, he anchors himself against the railing, staring at the sunset as it stares at him.

He shifts his weight towards the window, plopping his forehead on the glass. *Is my vision getting worse, or are the stars playing hide and seek in the pollution?*

The ding of the bell cuts the man’s internal dialogue short. He surges ahead, sailing up the flights of stairs adjacent to the elevator. On the way up, he encounters three pairs of titanium doors. Each blocks a new floor; however, his ID escorts him past them.

Behind the last set of doors is the roof, which greets Veryn with a sudden, chilling gust of wind. His body broaches to the wind by an inch, then rights itself. The teeth in his mouth start to dance with their own agenda, and his legs nearly fold to follow suit.

He steps off the circular platform and steers toward the metallic railing at the edge. Before he seats himself, however, the belated new-year lighting steals his gaze from hundreds of feet below. His jaw goes slack, his shoulders drop, and his pupils begin to drift outwards. Almost eight minutes pass before he shakes himself awake. *Not now*.

He hops to seat himself atop the railing, perching on the skyscraper like an eagle. His outerwear whips in the wind like wings, and his hair flutters like heavy feathers. He squints at the starless sky with a sour expression, every feature of his face scrunching to search for a dot of light; only black meets his eyes. Apricity embraces his form, though he speaks to it before reciprocating.

“How did they mess up the one view that was entirely meant for them?” he whispers to the Apricity.

He listens for a response, hands pocketed. Its zephyrs whistle back. His smile loses its teeth.

“But why lose what you gave them, in exchange for artificial warmth?”

The Apricity’s hands ruffle his hair, laughing with power that Veryn regards with a sneer. “The northerly winds aren’t toys,” he scolds. “And speak aloud, we’re alone up here.”

Its voice cries out in the wilderness, “Don’t be so quick to mistake my humor for hubris, old friend.”

Veryn rolls his eyes, leaning toward the street lights. “Your jokes haven’t gotten any better since our talk at the pond.”

The presence whistles into its only audience’s ears. “I’ll get a giggle out of you by the first prototype of the Dyson sphere.”

“You made that same claim during the Crusades.”

“Regarding what?”

“The Manhattan Project’s opus\!”

“Right, I did say that, didn’t I? The spacetime continuum is quite the athlete.”

Veryn chuckles, “And we bet on *them* winning the race.”

“By *them*, you mean humanity?”

“Of course.”

Veryn’s chest balloons with breath as a smirk finds his face. “You know, they’ve recently been pushing forward a newer title: amelioration,” he divulges.

The voice remains silent, and the gales slow to a breeze, as if to stop before crossing the street. From below, automobiles honk and the crowd mills about. Veryn nods and quiets himself as well, though he jumps at each crest in the acoustics.  

“I’m afraid that moniker eludes their current capabilities,” the presence resumes.

“Agreed.”

“But, it’s not a bad thing. It shows they’re still learning more about themselves, room to improve and all.”

“Did you expect them to plateau?”

“There was, is, and always will be that kind of possibility.”

“You were, are, and always will be worrying in that omnipresent tense, Pol—”

“Ah-ah, my name is forbidden now, remember?”

“By the Sovereigns, do you think *anyone* knowingly conveying your name correctly will cause such problems?”

“You remember the First Calamity, don’t you?”

Veryn raises an eyebrow. “And something of the sort will happen again?”

“Are you curious enough to find out?”

Veryn rolls his eyes, crossing his arms. “Be straight with me. What are all of these ridiculous battles you’re fighting even for?”

The man flicks his wrist at the city below them. The Apricity replies, “Are we not soldiers of our own cause?”

He snarls at the denizens of Manhattan. “Are we? *WE* are letting humanity tread this fatal trend, with impossible challenges dictating their lives rather than clear kindness. Truth be told, we—no, *you* have effaced yourself from the grand tapestry\! What from time immemorial made you so difficult?”

“It’s a ruinous saga of gods and monsters not worth explaining.”

The civil twilight dances in Veryn's eyes. Curtains close, and another act begins. A shift in the wind starts. This time, the sky watches the two directors. The first evening star—a star that isn’t a star—twinkles in the night sky. 

“…Do you see that ‘star’ in the sky? How it sits on stage by its lonesome?” the voice indicates. 

Veryn’s gaze travels up to the only dot of light populating the sky. “Yeah.”

“That celestial candle is lit for the first Sovereign of the second generation, Nora Zorana. She shall come from this post of yours—the pale blue dot—and I’m too sure of her ascension to heights far higher than this plane of existence permits.”

“Your point being?”

“Nora’s existence will be a great example of what you deemed impossible back then. I suggest you keep a close eye on her.”

“Ah, but what’s so special about a mortal like her, let alone a human? Her species only comprises loud, proud creatures.”

“They’re clandestine creatures that know how to cover their eyes. I assume you’ve never gotten close to one.”

“Not necessarily.”

“Then perhaps she will light the way into your heart, or at least shed some on the… *centerpiece* I’m hoping to draw.”

“Gatekeeping your plans as always\!” the ancient aide mocks. “But I’ll keep to your ‘wisdom.’”

Before Veryn can dismount the railing, a rumbling grows from the distance. The hairs on Veryn’s left arm stand at attention, and he extends his hand towards the source. It quietly declines until it’s no louder than the growling of Veryn’s stomach.

“The Calamity Colors, I presume?”

“Of course. It’s about time for them to coruscate again, the internet and all.”

“Right, we calculated that digital technology would be a calamity demanding their presence, as we did with all of the other calamities.”

Hearing those words, Veryn’s breathing shallows. His blinking slows, and the muscles around his eyes relax as its irises wander the world around him; he looks down, and starts to sway with the rhythm of Central Park’s trees. His eyes close, and time itself turns back to count the notches on the wall.

“Like that war betwixt the rivers.” He gesticulates, sketching an outline of Sumer in the air.

The trees below leap from the sway of a waltz to a jive. The metal Veryn sits atop creaks in tandem.

“Like that war with the wooden horse.”

Gusts of wind slam into Veryn, though he remains seated for their dialogue. Goosebumps form on his skin, though his words resonate with the same pitch and volume.

“Like the Crusades.”

Phantom sensations knock upon the door of Veryn’s Headspace. He invites them in like a fool would welcome vampires.

“Like those wars around the planet.”

The chambers of his heart reverberate with the roaring of men and machines. Ashes linger in the air. Copper stains his tongue. Armored men and steaming steel flash across his vision. His arm reaches up once more to hold a gloved hand, and his eyes moisten the moment their fingertips meet. He tilts forward, his hand almost wrapping around the other; yet, it pulls away and returns to the Ashes. He grabs at nothing, nearly plummeting into the street.

“Seems you’ve lost yourself too,” the wind whispers.

Veryn’s body wobbles headlong. “You won’t catch me, will you?” 

Gravel gathers in the voice as it speaks with the joy of a grieving father; each consonant grows rougher with every sentence.

“It’s time you learn to fly, and I’m not sure if I can help you like before, old friend. There is too much risk to go around.”

The man’s chest caves and shivers as he oscillates between concrete and air. A ringing sensation gores his ears with a pike. His hands flail and thrash about, as if he’s conducting an insubordinate orchestra. From his viewpoint, car lights, streetlights, and traffic lights melt into one amalgamation. 

“I’m not sure this is the right idea..\!”

A singular gust of wind strikes Veryn’s stern. He staggers forward, diving into the cityscape.

“Of course it is, just trust me\!”
