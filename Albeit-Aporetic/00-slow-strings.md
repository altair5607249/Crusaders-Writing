## Slow Strings

“This world we’ve created…” He pauses. The moment steals his voice as he takes it in. “It’s beautiful.”

“It is, isn’t it?”

“You know, you haven’t told me what it’s all for yet.”

“Well, Veryn, it’s the model that each individual’s SOL will innately know, so that they may choose to one day reach the grandeur of this place.”

“SOL… why must it reside within an individual?”

“No amount of space cannot accommodate each one’s vastness.”

Veryn points into the sky. “Forgive me if I lack the proper perspective, but… there’s infinite space.”

“Infinities of different magnitudes exist,” Polarys staunchly states. “It goes without saying.”

“...I fear there are limits to what these human brains can comprehend.”

“Excellent, because I can barely understand my own assertions,” Polarys snickers. “These details about humanity, especially their corporeal limitations, will inspire them to reach what they cannot with them in tow.”

“Oh, stop reveling in your machinations. We’ve gone over each of them in excruciating detail.”

“You say this, but never tire of the stars.”

Infantile dots of light crowd the ether, ignoring that emptiness that dictates them. Two directors of the scene—Polarys, the oldest dreamer, and Veryn, his aide—scrutinize the formation, comparing it to their blueprints of humanity’s home-to-be.

“The moment those stars rise to consciousness, *they* will be the first of humanity and the examples for posterity to follow, at least in terms of their linear time.” Polarys points skyward, his index finger tracing across each dot of light. “*They* are the **Sovereigns** of the celestial sphere surrounding us. You understand my plan, don’t you?”

“Of course,” Veryn nods. “I still wonder how this democracy can stand without your guidance, or at least some form of documentation behind your ‘grand tapestry.’”

“Maybe you’re underestimating their will to Overcome.”

“Even so, I fear you trust them *too* much.”

“Hm...”

Polarys gazes at the celestial sphere, running his pupils across the night and its candles. His hands clasp together at his waist, twitching with each twinkle from above. The stab of his teeth against his bottom lip leaves a trail of blood down his jaw. 

Veryn’s gaze drops from the sky, his body sinking into the ground they built together. His head rests on his palm as he ponders the pond in front of them. A reflection of the heavens gently greets him. “Are their posts being assigned to this planet?” he inquires.

“No, the Earth is in your hands.”

“What?\! I thought you said I’d be getting the most important post\!”

“Ah, were you under the assumption that the Highest Height would be your responsibility?”

“Obviously. We *both* know what that dreadful castle buries.”

“That place… it’s more suitable to house the Sovereigns. You’ll fare far better at the forefront of humanity.”

“But Pol—”

“I remembered every detail of your request before I came to an ultimatum. Rest assured, Earth is the perfect place for you.”

“How so?”

“It’s exactly as you want: a house that can be called so.”

“But don’t expect me to take shelter in any house,” Veryn replies. He pauses, turning to Polarys. “And like you said those eons ago, a house is not a home.”

“I’ve given you the scent of an explanation.”

“That’s called an excuse, dear friend.”

“Because the taste wouldn’t satisfy you, my friend. Though, if your curiosity remains, the answers will always be available in your observations.”

Veryn sighs, shaking his head. “Eyes do not feast.”

Polarys giggles, his stellar response already formed. “Are you sure? I feel my stomach filling with the stars right now\!”

“It’s infuriating how each utterance of yours sounds more passive than the last.”

“Maybe it’s better if you don’t understand if that’s enough to irk you.”

“I’m sure the act of leaving your words half-baked is enough to make anyone angry, is it not?”

Polarys’ head drifts down like a balloon that’s lost its air, passing its reflection in the pond before landing on his chest. The candles cease their chatter. They wait with bated breath for Polarys’ answer. Veryn, too, waits for an answer. Even Polarys waits.

“Veryn, the world will only meet your eyes halfway. It is up to you whether you bridge that remaining distance.” Polarys takes a step forward, a few centimeters closer to the water. “The same principle stands for when our worlds collide. To comprehend mine would require burning across an infinite distance.”

Veryn uproots himself from the ground with a frown. He puts a hand on Polarys’ right shoulder, insisting, “At the very least, I deserve to know. Knowledge is the most basic of freedoms you promised humanity would have, though your actions have been dictating otherwise ever since we engineered these heavy, sweaty bodies. Perhaps the protocols you’ve limited them with are more demanding than you initially thought.”

The contact between the two entities lingers amidst space’s simple symphony. The dreamer shifts to face his aide, his expression now illuminated by starlight. Polarys’ irises are whiter than a new star; Veryn recoils like he’d been blinded by one.

“…This corporeal form has stolen my sight. Thank you for returning a bit of it.”

“Is this why you’ve been hiding your eyes? So I wouldn’t catch your lies?”

Polarys laughs with a stationary gaze, letting a tear stain his right cheek. “And now they’re whiter than any lie I could tell. Still, I’d rather not burden you with the truth.”

Veryn scoffs. “No amount of truth can be held by one person.”

Polarys’ mouth hangs open, slowly curving into a crescent. “You remember.”

Veryn shrugs, his eyes wandering from Polarys’. “I’ll never forget it, especially after watching your live demonstration of that aphorism’s truth. To feel the apricity of one’s words means you’ve come down with the condition they create—those words were also yours.”

“Of course you remember that too. You’re much better than me at handling your Headspace, at least when ensconced in a mortal shell. Such is my reason for assigning you your post.”

“And why is that?”

“Ah, no other being will be able to protect the people here like you.”

“But the schematics for… *any* of the Sovereigns are excellent in that regard\!”

“And yet, they will grow beyond their schematics, much like you are already doing so with yours.”

“Then that implies every individual will eventually grow beyond their blueprint.”

“Precisely my point.”

Veryn’s eyes stir to the side, mixing with Polarys’. A maze of wrinkles gather around the aide’s brows. “…What?”

Polarys coughs out a chuckle before his eyes saunter away. “Veryn, I call my plan flawless because I’ve known all of its flaws. Every last one of those flaws is diminished and amplified by one fact: *Nothing* will have a place. It’s humanity’s willpower that will pave the final road.”

“To what?”

“Their resting place.”

“With what means? Will they move as a hivemind, the way those wicked monsters did?”

“Of course not. Each SOL is distinct for a reason.”

Veryn’s face contorts, his feelings waging wars on each other. “I never once took you for a liar, but this might finally be the moment I lose my faith in you\!”

“It sounds impossible, but you know as well as I do of what I gave up to promise humanity a way forward—beyond the script.”

“My faith in you,” Veryn mouths, his hand drifting down to the ground.

“Do you remember what I’ve prepared for them?”

“This is not the time for us to be joking so thoughtlessly.”

“Do you remember?”

“I want no part in this conversation.”

The north wind roars from Polarys’ bruised belly and moves through his bluish mouth in bursts, almost whisking him away even as it dies down. “So you do remember.”

Veryn bites the inside of his cheek, almost eating it before he can continue. 

“*Yourself*.”

“Indeed. 49% of myself to be exact.”

“You were always so reluctant to outright tell me what you’d accomplished through fragmentation, or where you’d put the fragment.”

“I’m sure it’s not hard to infer these answers now, is it?”

“But you’re practically praying for a miracle\!”

“What other choice do I have?” Polarys grins, rubbing the moisture from his clammy forehead. “My direct interference will only bring bedlam.”

Veryn shuts his eyes before blurting out, “Perhaps you could destroy the thing that’s forcing you to chase this final act.” He opens his eyes again to glance at Polarys.

The corners of Polarys’ eyes crinkle like paper, though Veryn scrutinizes them as if they’re soon to break. “Maybe it’s because I’m nearly blind, or because I’ve almost lost all other forms of sensation in this body, but I simply cannot see that happening. This risk is the only chance anyone has beyond the script, so it will be taken however it must be.”

Veryn’s hand returns to Polarys’ right shoulder. “For such a risky maneuver, you’ve been pulling the strings so slowly.”

“Slow strings make for good music,” the dreamer hums.

The aide huffs, sharing the dreamer’s subtle smile. The coolness of the cosmos washes over him as his shoulders release. His eyes return to the pond in front of them. It ebbs and flows as if to nod in acknowledgement at their conversation. He nods back, his eyes riding on the ripples in the water. *This makeshift sanctuary shall house our secrets*.

The heavens ponder the image trapped within the pond, watching as it winks whenever they wink. They extend their starlight to the water below, and so the water reaches back.  
   
Polarys’ eyes begin to reflect the sons and daughters of the ether. “Do you see it, Veryn?”

“See what?”

“Where earth ends, and heaven begins?”

They meet halfway with no delay, overlapping in a pantomime. Veryn’s pupils shake with effort, as if attempting to hold the whole world in a singular, mnestic frame. Instead, they settle on the dreamer and the stars in his smile.

“It’s like grabbing water in a dream, isn’t it?” Polarys jokes.

Veryn’s eyes inch open at his companion, scanning his arms, legs, face, and eyes until he could name each vein and its position. *Life-and-death companions, we said. I only hope our words hold true, lest I forever live in this moment*. A laugh that quivers like a cry escapes the aide.

“Yeah. Just ask me again, next eternity.”
